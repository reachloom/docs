Open a campaign and choose **Brief**. These details guide searches and emails.

- **What each email asks for:** one clear request for this campaign.
- **Who to contact:** the kinds of pages and people to look for.
- **Why they would care:** what their readers or team gain from your product.
- **Never contact:** competitors, kinds of sites, or people to leave alone.

## A campaign made from an idea

If the campaign was created from an idea under Content, the Brief has an **Idea** row at the top: a link with the idea's title that opens it. The campaign's header also shows a **From idea** badge, and the campaigns list an **Idea** chip.

The Brief itself starts as a copy of the idea's audience and angle and is **the campaign's own from then on**: change it here, and where it differs from the idea your Brief wins. Every campaign has a Brief, including ones you make by hand or over MCP, so it stays the one place to tune a campaign.

The idea still helps. When Reachloom finds pages and writes emails or answers, it also gets the idea's searches, its description of a good page, its reason, and what your own pages say. It does not get the AI's guesses ("assumed"), so a guess never ends up as a claim in an email. If you delete the campaign, the idea goes back to **New** and can be used again.

## Make it specific

A brief such as “developer blogs with tutorials about website monitoring” gives the search more direction than “anyone who needs our product”.

You can add email examples to guide the writing style. Use the [Automation](/tools/automation) steps for search exclusions, competitor sites, and instructions for first emails, replies, and follow-ups.

Edits guide future work. Check existing drafts separately before approving them.

## Keep exclusions together

The brief guides the AI's choices. **Settings → Exclusions** holds the workspace's blocked addresses and domains, including opt-outs. Use [Exclusions](/account/exclusions) for a contact that must stay blocked across campaigns.
